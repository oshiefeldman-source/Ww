Before the 2028 election

Five ways a prime minister leaves early

Anthony Albanese won a second term in May 2025. The next federal election is due by 2028. Here is how the job can change hands sooner.

Labor 94Crossbench 13Coalition 43
Route 1Resignation

He chooses to walk away

A PM can resign at any time. Labor elects a new leader and the Governor-General swears them in. No election is needed.

Decides
Albanese himself
Precedent
Robert Menzies retired mid-term in January 1966.
Route 2Leadership spill

His own party replaces him

Labor's 2013 rules make this hard: a spill against a sitting Labor PM needs a petition from 75% of caucus. Caucus and party members then vote 50/50.

Decides
Labor caucus and members
Precedent
Rudd 2010, Gillard 2013, Abbott 2015, Turnbull 2018.
Route 3No-confidence vote

The House turns on him

A government that loses a confidence vote must resign or go to an election. With 94 of 150 seats, at least 20 Labor MPs would have to vote against their own side.

Decides
House of Representatives
Precedent
Arthur Fadden's government fell on a budget vote in October 1941.
Route 4Disqualification

He is ruled ineligible

Under section 44 of the Constitution, the High Court can find an MP was never validly elected. Section 64 says a minister must hold a seat within three months.

Decides
High Court of Australia
Precedent
The 2017 citizenship crisis unseated Deputy PM Barnaby Joyce until a by-election.
Route 5Reserve powers

The Governor-General dismisses him

The Governor-General can withdraw a PM's commission. By convention this happens only in a constitutional crisis.

Decides
Governor-General
Precedent
Used once: Sir John Kerr dismissed Gough Whitlam on 11 November 1975.
